=====================================================================

                     ENGL 142N: SCIENCE IN LITERATURE

                 THE LITERARY HISTORY OF ARTIFICIAL INTELLIGENCE

=====================================================================

"In the beginning was the Word..."

There is no history of artificial intelligence without a literary
history of artificial intelligence.

Before AI was electronic, it was theological, philosophical,
mathematical, mechanical, and narrative.  This course traces a long
arc:

    LOGOS -> LOGIC -> CODE

Understanding these patterns is not antiquarian.
It may be useful.

---------------------------------------------------------------------
COURSE DESCRIPTION
---------------------------------------------------------------------

This course examines primary texts that imagine thinking matter,
symbolic systems, artificial beings, machinic memory, and networked
consciousness.  We treat AI not merely as technology, but as an
evolving literary structure.

From John 1:1 to Leibniz's calculus, from Poe's chess-playing
automaton to Searle's Chinese Room, from Bush's Memex to Nelson's
hypertext, and from Gibson's Wintermute to contemporary machine
learning imaginaries, we follow recurring metaphors that shape what
AI has been — and what it is becoming.

---------------------------------------------------------------------
GUIDING QUESTIONS
---------------------------------------------------------------------

* What does it mean for language to generate worlds — or machines?
* Can syntax think?
* Why do artificial beings first appear in literature?
* What is the difference between calculation and understanding?
* What patterns recur across centuries of symbolic thought?

---------------------------------------------------------------------
UNIT I — LOGOS AND SYMBOLIC INFINITY
---------------------------------------------------------------------

Primary Texts:

  - John 1:1–18
  - René Descartes, Discourse on Method (selections)
  - G.W. Leibniz on calculus and the Characteristica Universalis

Focus:

  Word as generative principle
  Infinity and the differential as compression
  The dream of a universal formal language

Pattern:

  INFINITY -> CALCULUS -> COMPUTATION

---------------------------------------------------------------------
UNIT II — AUTOMATA AND THEATRICAL INTELLIGENCE
---------------------------------------------------------------------

Primary Texts:

  - Edgar Allan Poe, "Maelzel's Chess-Player"
  - E.T.A. Hoffmann, "The Sandman"
  - Mary Shelley, Frankenstein (selections)

Focus:

  The automaton as interface
  Hidden labor and distributed cognition
  The uncanny as proto-AI anxiety

Pattern:

  AUTOMATON -> ALGORITHM -> AGENT

---------------------------------------------------------------------
UNIT III — SYNTAX, SEMANTICS, AND THE CHINESE ROOM
---------------------------------------------------------------------

Primary Texts:

  - Alan Turing, "Computing Machinery and Intelligence"
  - John Searle, "Minds, Brains, and Programs"
  - Norbert Wiener, Cybernetics (selections)

Focus:

  The imitation game as narrative experiment
  Syntax vs. semantics
  Systems, feedback, information

Question:

  Is the Chinese Room a philosophical argument —
  or a piece of speculative fiction?

---------------------------------------------------------------------
UNIT IV — MEMORY MACHINES AND HYPERTEXT DREAMS
---------------------------------------------------------------------

Primary Texts:

  - Vannevar Bush, "As We May Think"
  - Ted Nelson, Literary Machines (selections)
  - Jorge Luis Borges, "The Library of Babel"

Focus:

  The Memex as narrative architecture
  Hypertext as literary structure
  Infinite libraries and combinatorial worlds

Pattern:

  LIBRARY -> DATABASE -> NEURAL NETWORK

---------------------------------------------------------------------
UNIT V — NETWORKED CONSCIOUSNESS AND CORPORATE AI
---------------------------------------------------------------------

Primary Texts:

  - Philip K. Dick, Do Androids Dream of Electric Sheep? (sel.)
  - William Gibson, Neuromancer (Wintermute)
  - Donna Haraway, "A Cyborg Manifesto" (excerpt)

Focus:

  Distributed identity
  Corporate cognition
  Posthuman subjectivity

Pattern:

  DIVINE WORD -> FORMAL LANGUAGE -> MACHINE LEARNING MODEL

---------------------------------------------------------------------
MAJOR ASSIGNMENTS
---------------------------------------------------------------------

  1. Two analytical essays (4–5 pages)
  2. Structured debate or position paper
  3. Final research or creative-critical project

Possible directions:

  * Trace a metaphor across centuries (Word, Machine, Library,
    Ghost, Network)
  * Design a conceptual Memex interface with annotations
  * Write short speculative fiction with critical reflection

---------------------------------------------------------------------
LEARNING OUTCOMES
---------------------------------------------------------------------

Students will:

  - Analyze foundational texts shaping ideas of AI
  - Identify recurring metaphors across centuries
  - Produce rigorous analytical writing
  - Develop creative or hybrid scholarly projects

---------------------------------------------------------------------

Understanding the literary history of AI clarifies the future of AI.
